# Open Problem 5: A New Problem, Still in the Wrong Place

Everyday Workplace Communication · Module 1 · Segment 6: Finding Information · **Stage 6.2** (COM-M1-OP-05), in the Swift AI Academy frame.

The whole activity is one plain HTML page (`index.html`), with its pictures and sounds inside the file. No framework or build step is needed. `Module1_Open_Problem_5_Stage6.2_Still_in_the_Wrong_Place.html` is the same file, kept for downloading. It opens with a double-click.
The page is generated from `module1-segment1/` with `node scripts/plain-page.mjs scripts/op62-plain.template.html <out.html>`. Do not edit it by hand.

## Publish on GitHub + Vercel

1. In Vercel choose **Add New → Project** and import this repository.
2. Set **Framework Preset** to **Other**. Leave the build command and output directory empty.
3. Choose **Deploy**.

GitHub Pages also works: Settings → Pages → deploy from the `main` branch, root folder.

## How it works

1. **Your next move.** The opening story uses the three painted scenes from the client's asset package, with soft cross-fades:
   - Ananya, seen from behind, checks the list on the notice board, holding her note and her admission folder;
   - she compares the list with her note, and it does not clear things up;
   - she stays at the board, with a thought bubble of question marks.

   The narrator reads each caption as it types in, and Ananya says "Hmm. I am still not sure this is the right place." The story plays once, on its own where the browser allows sound. Browsers block sound until the visitor taps or presses a key, so otherwise the story runs silently and the first tap starts it again with sound. The sound button sits in the caption bar, so it never covers the picture. People who prefer reduced motion see the last scene.

   Then the script's question: "You checked the list, but you are still not sure you are in the right place. What now?" The learner writes their next move (5 to 50 words). A reason is welcome but not required. An optional "Need help getting started?" panel gives the package's three hints, which do not suggest an answer.
2. **Compare and reflect.** The learner sees their answer and a short feedback message. The message is built from an internal check: a sensible next move (several are accepted: ask someone, check the list or the note again, look for other signs, call or message someone, or wait for someone) and a finished sentence ("I would ask" must say who). A reason is praised when given. **The key words and the checks are never shown to learners**; staff can find them in the separate checking-metric document. Then comes the script's feedback, as a thought to take forward: "When checking alone does not resolve it, asking someone directly is the natural next step." It is labelled as a next step to consider, not a mark against the answer. Optional voice practice is available, and the learner can choose "Try another answer".
3. **From checking to asking.** The package's question: "What would you want someone to clarify?" (3 to 30 words). It is checked internally for one thing to clarify: where to go or which room, the list or the note, the class or its time, or whether the learner is in the right place. A short phrase such as "Which room my class is in." is accepted here. Then come ideas to compare.

At the end: "Practice complete", "Next in the course: Segment 7, Asking a Stranger for Help.", **Download my notes** (a text file) and **Delete my saved work**.

- **Only real answers are accepted.** Before any feedback, the page checks that the answer is a real answer to the question. It must be mostly real English words; its content words must mostly be real everyday words or words from the question; it must not repeat the same word or be just "I do not know"; it must be written as a sentence (a short phrase is enough in step 3); and it must be on the question. Otherwise the learner stays on the question with a short message ("This does not answer the question yet…"), and the attempt is saved in the record as not valid. For example, "apple orange mango yes and" and "I would eat a mango because it is sweet" are not accepted. Weak but genuine answers, such as "I would just guess a room", are still accepted and get gentle feedback.
- **Not graded:** a "Practice · not graded" badge and no score. Attempts are saved in the browser as `6.2.open-problem-log`. Nothing is sent anywhere; recordings are never saved or uploaded.
- The top bar shows "About 12 minutes" (the stage's own time) and never counts down. There is no Pause button and no time limit, as in the other Module 1 pages.
- No contractions are used anywhere on screen.
